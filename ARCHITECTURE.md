# Architecture

`payment-recovery-agent` is a CLI that takes failed Razorpay payment events and decides, per event, whether a recovery is possible and what it is: a fresh Payment Link with a customer message, a saved-token retry (stubbed), or nothing at all with a person in the loop. It was built in a four-hour timebox for Razorpay's AI Buildathon (Track 03) and extended afterwards; the extensions are documented under `docs/` and summarised in decisions D7 to D10 below. Classification of known error objects, the policy table, the retry schedule, the timing rules and the reminder cadence are deterministic; a model is consulted in exactly two places (an error description no rule matched, and the wording of the customer nudge) and both places degrade to a fixed fallback. Every outbound call is guarded by an idempotency key that is UNIQUE in the database and inserted before the call, and by a stop that fires the moment the order is paid another way. Deliberately cut: rail-switch logic, a delivery provider of our own for the drafted wording (Razorpay's own link notification is the only thing that reaches a customer, and only when `RAZORPAY_NOTIFY_CUSTOMER=1`), real token retries (recorded as `stubbed`), a real queue (a poll loop over one table stands in), schema migrations (`create_all`), and live validation of the Razorpay API shapes this build assumes. Events arrive from `scripts/seed.py`, from `app/ingest.py` (`payment.failed`, `subscription.pending` / `subscription.halted`, the stdlib `serve-webhook` receiver, or the dashboard's CSV export) or as rows from `scripts/chaos.py`; the receiver has been driven by tests and local requests only, never by a live Razorpay delivery. With no keys in `.env`, Razorpay is an in-memory fixture and the LLM is absent; the README says exactly what that changes.

## Pipeline

One event runs through `app/pipeline.py:process_attempt` in this order and no other. Each stage writes at least one row to `audit_log` through `app/models.py:audit` (the execute stage writes one per HTTP attempt plus one for the result; the ingest row comes from `scripts/seed.py` for seeded events and from the pipeline for raw events).

IST is a fixed `+05:30` in `app/scheduling.py`, used only to decide the salary window and quiet hours.

## Data model

Five tables in `app/models.py`. Plain foreign keys with `PRAGMA foreign_keys=ON` (set in `app/db.py`). The ORM relationships from `PaymentAttempt` to decisions, jobs and outcomes carry `cascade="all, delete-orphan"`; `audit_log` has no cascade, so deleting an attempt through the ORM removes its decisions/jobs/outcomes but not its audit rows.

| table | one row per | why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_attempts` | failed payment event | Razorpay's error object verbatim (`error_code`, `error_source`, `error_step`, `error_reason`, `error_description`), the amount in paise, `has_token`, the customer's contact/email and `customer_language` (from `notes.language`, the CSV's `language` column, else empty), `failed_at`. `razorpay_payment_id` is UNIQUE, so a redelivered event finds its own row. `order_paid_at` and `paid_by_payment_id` are set on every row sharing an `order_id` when a capture arrives, so the guard is one column read, not a join. `subscription_id`, `subscription_status` (`pending` / `halted`) and `subscription_url` come from the `subscription.*` webhooks. |
| `recovery_decisions` | decision taken for an attempt (a redelivery writes a second, identical one) | `failure_class`, `action`, `delay_seconds`, `max_attempts`, `reason` (classification reason and policy rationale, joined with ` \| `), `classified_by` (`rules` / `llm` / `fallback`), `llm_used`, `llm_model`, `llm_latency_ms`, `confidence`, `fallback_taken`. This is where "was a model involved, and did it fail" is answered per event. |
| `recovery_jobs` | scheduled recovery action, or a reminder of one | `idempotency_key` UNIQUE (`uq_recovery_jobs_idempotency_key`), `retry_seq`, `action` (the table's actions, or `reminder`), `scheduled_at`, `schedule_note` (why `scheduled_at` is not `now + delay`: `salary window: 2 Oct 10:00 IST`, `quiet hours: ...`), `status` (`pending`, `executing`, `sent`, `failed`, `human_queue`, `no_action`, `stubbed`, `cancelled`; `skipped_duplicate` is reported for a redelivery but never stored, since the existing row is returned untouched), `razorpay_link_id`, `razorpay_link_url`, `link_source` (`payment_link`, or `subscription_url` for a halted mandate's own page, created by nobody), `parent_job_id` (a reminder points at its link job), `attempts_made` (HTTP attempts, committed before each call), `last_error` (for a failed link job `<kind>: <detail>` with kind in `local_guard`, `final_4xx`, `rate_limited`, `server_error`, `transport`, `client_error`, which is what `finish_job` routes on; `order already paid by <id>` or `contact cap: ...` for a parked one), the drafted `nudge_*` fields, `executed_at`. A `do_orm_execute` listener adds `action != "reminder"` to every SELECT of this table, including the `PaymentAttempt.jobs` relationship, unless the statement carries `with_reminders(stmt)`: every reader that takes "the latest job" as the attempt's status keeps seeing the link, and the scheduler, the contact cap, the paid-elsewhere stop and `cadence` ask for reminders explicitly. |
| `outcomes` | closed loop for an attempt | `recovered`, `recovered_at`, `amount_recovered_paise` (what the link actually collected), `note` (`payment_link.paid via poll (plink_...)`, `payment link expired (...)`, `payment link cancelled (...)`, `order paid elsewhere: no recovery needed`, `human_queue: ...`, `no_action: ...`, `token_retry stubbed: ...`, `resolved by a person: ...`), `job_id`. Every job that cannot recover anything gets one; a reminder never gets one (the outcome belongs to the link); only a sent link waits for `poll` or a webhook. |
| `audit_log` | stage per attempt, append-only | `stage` (`ingest`, `classify`, `llm`, `policy`, `schedule`, `execute`, `reconcile`, `nudge`, `deliver`, `cancel`, `poll`, `outcome`), a one-line `message`, `data_json`. |

What the audit trail answers, for one attempt, from `python -m app.main audit --attempt <id>`: what the event said when it arrived; which rule fired, or that none did; whether a model was asked, which one, how many milliseconds it took, and how it failed if it did; which action and delay the table chose and why, and whether a merchant file or a subscription state changed it; which idempotency key the job was inserted under, or that the key was already present; whether and why the send time moved; every HTTP attempt, the status it got, how long the backoff was, and which client answered (`"client": "fixture"` vs `"fault:razorpay_429"` in `data_json`); that a message was drafted, from which source, and in which language; what Razorpay was asked to deliver and its receipt, or why nothing was; when each reminder was scheduled, sent, moved, parked or voided; what Razorpay said the link became, or that the order was paid another way and the link cancelled. The chaos harness builds its "path taken" lines from these rows rather than from its own bookkeeping.

## Decisions

### D1. Deterministic classification for known error objects

Decision: rules, not a model. `app/classify.py` evaluates thirteen `Rule` entries in a fixed order (`risk_flag`, `hard_decline_structured`, `hard_decline_reason`, `hard_decline_description`, `insufficient_funds`, `limit_exceeded_structured`, `limit_exceeded_description`, `auth_abandoned_structured`, `auth_abandoned_description`, `issuer_down_structured`, `issuer_down_description`, `network_timeout_structured`, `network_timeout_description`); first match wins; anything unmatched is `UNKNOWN` by construction, and only `UNKNOWN` reaches `llm.classify_unmapped`. Rules key on the structured fields first and on word-boundary regexes over the lowercased description second, so `otp` does not match `footprint` and a risk or fraud word wins over anything else in the same text. Measured on the build machine: about 26 microseconds per event over the original 20 seed events (median of five runs of 500 x 20 with `time.perf_counter`; a one-off measurement, not a benchmark).

Alternatives rejected:
- A model for every event. Non-deterministic in a money path (the same error object can get a different class tomorrow), a network round trip with an 8-second timeout (`LLM_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`) where the rules take microseconds, and a per-token bill for reading a finite enumerated set. Razorpay's `code`/`source`/`step`/`reason` are a closed vocabulary; a lookup does not get more accurate by being clever.
- A flat `error_code -> class` dictionary. The structured fields alone are ambiguous: `card_declined` covers "card has expired" (HARD_DECLINE) and "insufficient balance" (INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS); `payment_timed_out` is abandonment at `payment_authentication` and a network timeout at `payment_authorization`. The rules need order and both the fields and the description.
- A fine-tuned small classifier. No labelled data, four hours, and a class must be explainable in the audit trail; `Classification.reason` is `rule <name>: <what it checks>`, which a reviewer can read and a classifier cannot produce.

Consequence: the rules are deliberately conservative. A bare "declined by the issuing bank", a Hinglish SBI message and an ISO 8583 "05 Do not honour" (three of the twenty seed events) fall through, and a non-Latin-script description contributes nothing to the keyword rules (the regexes are ASCII), so such an event is classified only by its structured fields (code, source, step, reason) or falls through, so the unmapped path has to exist and has to be safe: it needs the confidence threshold and the human queue in D2, and it carries all of that traffic. The rules key on the reason values we had to hand (`card_declined`, `payment_cancelled`, `payment_timed_out`, `bank_technical_error`, `bank_failure`, `gateway_technical_error`, and any reason containing `risk`, `fraud` or `insufficient`) plus description keywords; Razorpay's full reason vocabulary could not be verified offline, so a real description with none of the listed words is `UNKNOWN` on purpose and not tested against live webhook payloads. The rules are scored by `scripts/eval_classify.py` over 118 hand-labelled cases (`tests/fixtures/failure_cases.json`; `docs/classifier_eval.md`): 0 wrong and 100% coverage of the cases that have a class, pinned by `tests/test_eval_classify.py`. That is a regression floor on the tuning set, not production accuracy. Tuning against the set also surfaced a latent bug in the first cut, a `GATEWAY_ERROR` comparison against a lowercased field that could never match, and added the eighth class, `LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (a daily or per-transaction cap, which clears on a schedule and is neither an empty account nor a dead card). The same labelled set now supplies the few-shot examples in the model's cached classification prompt (two per class, chosen by sorted id; `docs/llm.md`), and recurring unmapped descriptions come back to it as `Rule(...)` stubs from `propose-rules` (D9), so the rules grow from evidence a person reviewed and never from the model's own habit.

### D2. Policy as a table, not a model

Decision: `POLICY` in `app/policy.py` is an eight-row dict a payments reviewer can read in one screen, and `decide()` is a pure function over it: (1) a classification whose `source` is not `rules` must have `confidence >= config.LLM_MIN_CONFIDENCE` (0.7) or the action is `human_queue`; (2) `RISK_BLOCKED` and `UNKNOWN` are terminal (`human_queue`, `max_attempts` 0) and ignore `retry_seq`; (3) `retry_seq > max_attempts` returns `no_action` with a rationale starting `stop rule: max attempts reached`; (4) otherwise the table, after a merchant's overrides (D10), with `token_action` substituted when `has_token`, `delay = round(base * backoff ** (seq - 1))`, and `nudge` forced off for a token retry (the customer has nothing to do); (5) a link action for an event with neither contact nor email is `human_queue` (`reachable=False`), because a link nobody receives is not a recovery, while a token retry needs no channel. A fallback classification carries `confidence 0.0`, so `llm_timeout`, `llm_bad_json`, `llm_hallucinated_class` and `llm_unavailable` all land in the human queue through the same gate rather than through special cases; a valid low-confidence answer (0.55 in `tests/test_pipeline.py`) is stored with the model's real number and gated the same way. One override sits outside the table on purpose: `cadence.subscription_override` parks a `subscription.pending` charge (Razorpay is still retrying it) unless the class is HARD_DECLINE or RISK_BLOCKED, and turns a `subscription.halted` decision into the subscription's own re-authorisation URL with no Payment Link created.

| class | action | with saved token | delay | backoff | max attempts | nudge | why (rationale strings verbatim in `app/policy.py`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS | recovery_link | recovery_link | 48h | x1 | 2 | yes | balance replenishes on salary cycles; a 10-minute retry burns an issuer attempt |
| ISSUER_DOWN | recovery_link | token_retry | 15m | x3 (15m, 45m, 2h15m) | 3 | link only | transient outage; back off threefold if it stays down |
| AUTH_ABANDONED | recovery_link | recovery_link | 10m | x1 | 2 | yes | intent existed, OTP/3DS friction killed it; nothing retries without the customer |
| HARD_DECLINE | nudge_change_method | nudge_change_method | now | x1 | 1 | yes | a blocked, expired or invalid card can never authorise; ask for a different method |
| RISK_BLOCKED | human_queue | human_queue | now | - | 0 | no | never auto-retry a risk flag; a retry is fraud-adjacent |
| NETWORK_TIMEOUT | recovery_link | token_retry | 5m | x2 (5m, 10m) | 2 | link only | likely transient; double the wait if it recurs |
| LIMIT_EXCEEDED | recovery_link | recovery_link | 24h | x1 | 2 | yes | a daily or per-transaction cap clears when its window resets; an immediate retry, token or not, burns an attempt against the same cap |
| UNKNOWN | human_queue | human_queue | now | - | 0 | no | no rule matched and the model did not resolve it with confidence; a person decides |

Alternatives rejected:
- Let the model choose the action and the delay. A prompt cannot be reviewed the way eight rows can, "retry a risk-flagged card" has to be impossible rather than unlikely, and the audit trail would show a sentence where a payments team needs a rule.
- A learned policy tuned on recovery rates (bandit over class x delay). There is no production data, only a hand-specified simulation (`scripts/simulate.py`); it would need weeks of traffic and a guardrail layer that looks exactly like this table anyway. What exists instead is D9: a report that says when the data would support a change, and a person who makes it.
- A per-merchant configurable rules engine. More surface than the timebox allowed. The per-merchant layer that exists now (D10) is deliberately narrower than an engine: a JSON file that can tighten a row and never loosen it.

Consequence: the human queue is a first-class outcome, not an error. With no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, 5 of the 22 seed events end there (2 RISK_BLOCKED, 3 UNKNOWN) and an `Outcome` row is written at decision time saying so. The table is also what `scripts/simulate.py` runs, so the baseline comparison measures the code that ships. Nothing tunes itself: a recurring unmapped description becomes a new rule in `app/classify.py`, not a habit. Two design choices worth knowing: INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS never token-retries even with a token (an unannounced debit into an empty account is a bounce for the customer and a return for the merchant), and lost/stolen cards are HARD_DECLINE unless a risk word is present, in which case `risk_flag` wins because it is first.

### D3. Recovery is a new Payment Link, not a re-charge

Decision: you cannot re-charge a failed card without a saved token or an active mandate, so the executor's real action is `POST /payment_links` (`app/executor.py:build_payment_link_payload`): amount in paise, `reference_id = idempotency_key[:40]`, the customer's name/contact/email when present, `notify` and `reminder_enable` only when `RAZORPAY_NOTIFY_CUSTOMER=1` and the merchant file has not set `notify_customer: false` (off by default: the seed contacts look real and a live test run would message them), `expire_by = max(now, wall clock) + max(1h, RECOVERY_LINK_EXPIRY_HOURS)` (72h by default; `run-due --now` replays a clock and must not produce an expiry in the past), `notes` carrying `payment_id`, `order_id`, `failure_class`, `retry_seq` and `agent`, plus `callback_url` only when `CALLBACK_URL` is set. `token_retry` is chosen by the table only for ISSUER_DOWN and NETWORK_TIMEOUT when `has_token`, and `execute_job` records it as `stubbed` with zero outbound calls and the audit line `STUB: would charge saved token via Orders+Payments API (recurring); no real tokens in test mode`. For a halted subscription the "link" is the subscription's own `short_url` from the payload (`link_source = subscription_url`) and no create call is made.

Alternatives rejected:
- Re-charge through Orders + Payments with the saved instrument. Not possible without a token or mandate, and there are no real tokens in test mode inside four hours; pretending with a fake token would demo a path that does not exist. The stub says what it would do and why it does not.
- Re-present the mandate for every class that has a token. Even with a token, INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS is a link plus a nudge at 48 hours by design (`token_action: None` in the table); a silent debit into an empty account costs the customer a fee and recovers nothing.
- Send the customer back to the original checkout page. There is no server or checkout in scope; a Payment Link is a hosted page Razorpay renders, reminds about and expires, and its `reference_id` doubles as the API-side duplicate guard in D4.

Consequence: a sent link is never re-issued while it is open. The customer holds it, and the reminder cadence (D8) re-notifies the same link through `notify_by`; a second live link for one order is a double-payment risk. A follow-up under a new key (`pipeline.schedule_followup`) exists in exactly two cases: an outbound failure that provably did not land (a rate limit, or an ambiguous failure that `executor.reconcile_failed_job` has shown created no link under the job's `reference_id`), and a link that expired unpaid (`payment_link.expired`, or `poll` seeing `expired`), where the class's next delay counts from the expiry time until the stop rule closes the payment (`pipeline.schedule_expiry_followup`; `tests/test_money_path.py::test_expired_link_mints_the_next_attempt_until_the_stop_rule`). An ambiguous failure whose lookup finds the link becomes `sent`; one the lookup cannot resolve, a final 4xx and a local guard all go to a person with no automatic retry. A `stubbed` token retry gets no follow-up because a stub cannot fail, and gets a `token_retry stubbed` outcome so it does not sit open. The token path is visible as `stubbed` in `show` and is the first real feature to build (Razorpay tokens and recurring APIs, plus a mandate check before any debit).

### D4. Idempotency key with a DB UNIQUE constraint, checked before the outbound call, echoed as reference_id

Decision: `idempotency_key = sha256(f"{razorpay_payment_id}:{retry_seq}")`, UNIQUE in `recovery_jobs`, inserted and committed by `executor.schedule_job` before anything is sent. Four layers; the first three are tested in `tests/test_idempotency.py`, the fourth in `tests/test_executor.py` and `tests/test_pipeline.py`: (1) the INSERT fails with `IntegrityError` on a redelivery, the session rolls back, a `skipped_duplicate` audit row is written and the existing job is returned untouched; (2) `execute_job` claims the row with an atomic compare-and-set (`UPDATE ... WHERE status = 'pending'` -> `executing`) and refuses `sent`, `stubbed`, `executing`, `cancelled`, `human_queue` and `no_action` before any call, so a second worker that races in is turned away; (3) the first 40 characters of the key go to Razorpay as `reference_id`, and a 400 whose description says the reference already exists makes the executor look the link up by that `reference_id` and record it as `sent (already existed)`, or park the job for a person if it cannot be found, never create a second link; (4) a failed job whose last error was ambiguous is reconciled by the same lookup before the policy chain may mint a follow-up under a new key, and a follow-up itself re-checks any earlier ambiguous attempt whose request may have landed late. An event delivery always uses `retry_seq = 1`, so it collides whether or not the first job already executed; only the scheduler and the expiry path mint higher sequences (`executor.next_retry_seq`). A reminder carries its own key, `sha256(f"{payment_id}:{retry_seq}:reminder:{n}")`, under the same constraint, so a redelivered event or a second `finish_job` creates no second cadence.

Alternatives rejected:
- Rely on the API alone. Razorpay's docs were unreachable from the build box, so whether the real API rejects a duplicate `reference_id`, with which wording, and whether `GET /payment_links?reference_id=` lists links by reference are assumptions (listed as such in `FixtureRazorpayClient`'s docstring; the wording is matched loosely by `_is_duplicate_reference`, and a lookup that answers differently parks the payment for a person). The database constraint holds regardless; the API is the third layer, not the first.
- An in-memory set of seen payment ids. It dies with the process, and a redelivery after a restart is exactly what an at-least-once webhook produces; it is also invisible to a second worker.
- A distributed lock (Redis `SETNX`, an advisory lock). One more service and one more failure mode; a lock says "one at a time", the key says "at most once, ever", which is the property that matters for money, and the UNIQUE constraint gives it with no extra infrastructure.

Consequence: the job row must be durable before the HTTP call, so a crash between the call and the `sent` write leaves a committed `pending` row that a redelivery skips and `run-due` re-executes when due, where it hits layer 3 and recovers the link; a lost HTTP answer hits layer 4 (the `db_unavailable` scenario in `scripts/chaos.py` probes both). A redelivered UNKNOWN event still pays for its LLM classification before the key rejects the job, because the check happens at schedule time by design. Two processes executing one pending job at the same instant are turned away by the atomic `pending` -> `executing` claim (layer 2) rather than both reaching the outbound call; a crash mid-execution leaves the row `executing`, which `run-due` reclaims and reconciles by `reference_id`. A true multi-worker deployment still wants a lease with a timeout on `executing` (part of D6's migration); the `reference_id` guard is the backstop.

### D5. Direct REST client instead of the official SDK

Decision: `app/razorpay_client.py` talks to `https://api.razorpay.com/v1` with `urllib.request`, HTTP Basic auth and JSON, and raises `RazorpayError(status, code, description, details)` on any non-2xx, `status 0` with code `NETWORK` on a transport failure, and `BAD_RESPONSE` on a non-JSON 2xx. `LiveRazorpayClient`, `FixtureRazorpayClient` (in-memory, deterministic ids derived from `reference_id`) and `FaultingRazorpayClient` (429 twice then success; 502 always; cancels and notifies passed through) implement one `Protocol` of six calls (create, fetch and list Payment Links, cancel, `notify_by`, list an order's payments), so the executor never knows which it is talking to. The backoff policy in `executor.execute_job` keys on the status: 429, 5xx and 0 retry with `0.5s, 1.0s` between three attempts; any other 4xx is final.

Alternatives rejected:
- The official `razorpay` package as-is. Checked against `razorpay==2.0.1` from PyPI: `razorpay/client.py:request()` reads the error body and picks the exception class from the body's `error.code` string (`BAD_REQUEST_ERROR` -> `BadRequestError`, `GATEWAY_ERROR` -> `GatewayError`, everything else -> `ServerError`), and every class in `razorpay/errors.py` takes only a message. The HTTP status is gone: a 429 raises whichever class its body code names, indistinguishable from a validation error the executor must never retry, and a 500 and a 502 are the same `ServerError`. A non-JSON error body (a proxy's HTML 502) fails inside `response.json()`, the SDK prints its own retry progress to stdout, and it pulls in `requests`.
- The SDK with a patched session to capture the status. Monkey-patching a third-party client's internals in a money path is worse than a short `urllib` client a reviewer can read end to end, and it keeps the stdout prints and the extra dependency.
- `httpx` or `requests` directly. One more dependency for a handful of endpoints; the stdlib is enough, and the Protocol is what makes the fixture and the fault wrapper share the executor's code path.

Consequence: the live path is unit-tested against a fake `urlopen` in `tests/test_razorpay_client.py` and `tests/test_cadence.py` and has never hit the real API from this environment (network blocked, no keys); the request and response shapes follow the facts we had, and the fixture's docstring lists precisely which behaviours are assumed, now including the cancel, order-payments and `notify_by` shapes. Test-mode-only is enforced twice: `config.razorpay_live()` raises on a key that does not start with `rzp_test_`, and `LiveRazorpayClient.__init__` refuses the same.

### D6. SQLite and an in-process scheduler for the timebox

Decision: SQLAlchemy with `sqlite:///./recovery.db` by default (`app/db.py`), tables created by `Base.metadata.create_all` with no migrations, and `app/scheduler.py:run_due` as the scheduler: select `pending` jobs, reminders included, with `scheduled_at <= now` ordered by `(scheduled_at, id)`, execute each with one Razorpay client per sweep (so the fault wrapper's "429 twice then success" counter spans the sweep; a real rate limit applies per request whatever client object sends it), draft the nudge, record delivery and schedule the cadence for a sent link, reconcile or schedule the next `retry_seq` for a failed one. Follow-ups and reminders are never due in the same sweep because every delay and every reminder offset is positive, so one call terminates. `docker-compose.yml` offers Postgres behind a `DATABASE_URL` change (`psycopg2-binary` deliberately not pinned by default).

Alternatives rejected:
- Postgres from the start. `make demo` has to run from a clean clone with nothing but `.env`, and a container is a question a reviewer has to ask; SQLAlchemy keeps the swap to one environment variable.
- A real queue (SQS, Kafka, Celery, RQ). A broker to run, nothing to demonstrate about it inside four hours, and the job row and its `reference_id` already exist before any call, so two workers on one job are refused by the status re-read or, if they race past it, by Razorpay's duplicate-`reference_id` rejection (assumed, not verified; a lease on `execute_job` is part of the migration), which makes the migration safe to postpone.
- Alembic migrations. There is no schema history to manage yet; `create_all` is the honest first cut, and the cut is stated in `db.init_db`'s docstring rather than hidden. The columns added since (`order_paid_at`, `schedule_note`, `parent_job_id`, `subscription_*`, `customer_language`, ...) were added to the model only; an existing database file has to be recreated, which is the first thing Alembic would fix.

Consequence: this is the answer to "what breaks at 10,000 merchants": the scheduler. One process scans one table for due rows and executes them sequentially with real sleeps (1.5 s per rate-limited job with the default constants); there is no lease, no worker pool, no partitioning by merchant, and SQLite serialises every writer. The migration path is Postgres with a `SKIP LOCKED` worker pool, or a queue fed by the same `schedule_job` rows, and it does not change the safety argument: `execute_job` refuses executed rows before any call and the `reference_id` catches the race after it. The database boundary is the one place the pipeline raises on purpose (`PipelineDBError`, exit code 2), because an unacknowledged event is redelivered and a link without a record is not.

### D7. Stop on paid-elsewhere, layered like idempotency

Decision: a recovery link is a second way to pay the same order, so the moment the order is paid another way every pending recovery is noise and every live link is a double-payment risk. The stop is four layers that mirror D4 (`docs/money_path.md`, section 1): (1) the record: `payment.captured` and `order.paid` webhooks (and `payment_link.paid` for a sibling attempt) set `order_paid_at` / `paid_by_payment_id` on every attempt sharing the `order_id`, the earliest capture winning; (2) the guards: `schedule_job` refuses a new row (`no_action`, `last_error = order already paid by <id>`, outcome written, zero calls) and `execute_job` re-reads the columns before the atomic claim, token retries included, because charging a token for an order the customer has since paid is the double charge the agent exists to avoid; (3) the cancel: `ingest.record_order_paid` voids pending jobs and reminders and cancels a live link with one `POST /payment_links/{id}/cancel`, the job ending `cancelled` with its outcome; a cancel that fails or answers anything but `status: cancelled` parks the job `human_queue` naming the live link, never a silent live link, and a redelivered capture finds nothing to do; (4) the belt and braces: with real test keys (`CHECK_ORDER_BEFORE_SEND=auto`) `execute_job` asks `GET /orders/{id}/payments` before every create and stops on a captured payment it was never told about, while a failed check is audited and the send proceeds because the webhook is the primary record. The ninth chaos fault, `order_paid_elsewhere`, runs all four.

Alternatives rejected:
- Rely on the webhook alone and let a late link expire. A link that outlives its order is live for up to 72 hours; a customer who pays it has paid twice, and a refund is a support ticket the agent caused.
- Rely on the order check alone. It is one more call per send, it cannot run against the fixture (which has no orders), and a call that fails would either block every send or silently pass; the webhook record is durable and free.
- Cancel through `poll` on its next sweep. Minutes to hours of exposure, and `poll` only asks about links; it would never learn about a pending job for the same order.

Consequence: `cancelled` is a terminal job status, a `cancel` audit stage exists, and `open_link_jobs` never returns a cancelled link, so `poll` stops asking about it. The cancel and order-payments shapes are assumptions (section 5 of the document); a wrong assumption costs one human review, never a second link. The order check sets `order_paid_at` to the check time, not the capture time, because the payments list is not read for one.

### D8. Timing is a table of deterministic rules, not a model

Decision: when a link goes out is decided by three pure functions of `now` in `app/scheduling.py`, applied in one place (`executor.schedule_job`) to link jobs and reminders, never to a token retry: an INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS send that would land on the 24th to the 31st (IST) moves to day `SALARY_WINDOW_DAY` (2) of the next month at 10:00 IST, a heuristic about Indian salary credits stated as such in the module; any customer send inside 21:00 to 09:00 IST moves to the next 09:00 IST; and a customer phone or email that already received `MAX_CONTACTS_PER_CUSTOMER_PER_WEEK` (3, lowerable per merchant) link or reminder sends in a rolling 7 days gets `human_queue` instead of another (`docs/money_path.md`, section 3). The reminder cadence (`app/cadence.py`) is a per-class tuple of offsets after the send (INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS +24h and +60h, AUTH_ABANDONED +2h and +24h, the other link classes +24h, RISK_BLOCKED and UNKNOWN none), each a `RecoveryJob` with `action = reminder`, its own key and `parent_job_id`, moved out of quiet hours, skipped past the link's expiry, counted against the cap, executed by `run-due` as `notify_by` per medium with the receipt in a `deliver` row, and voided the moment the link is paid, expired or cancelled (`docs/delivery.md`). Every move is on the row as `schedule_note` and in the trail as `send time moved from ... to ... (<note>)`; a delay-0 class whose send quiet hours moved waits for `run-due` like any other job (`tests/test_money_path.py::test_quiet_hours_are_honoured_on_the_process_path_for_a_delay_zero_class`), and only `--execute-now` runs ahead, saying so.

Alternatives rejected:
- ML-timed retries per card, the industry's headline feature (Stripe, Recurly, Chargebee Revive, Paddle, Butter in `docs/landscape.md`). It needs cross-merchant outcome data this agent will never have, it cannot be reviewed the way three rules can, and the part a merchant-side agent could honestly learn from its own outcomes is exactly what D9 reports instead of applying.
- No contact cap. Two follow-ups plus two reminders per link, across sibling attempts for one customer, is a dozen messages in a week; the cap is what makes the cadence safe to ship on by default, and a parked fourth send is a person's decision, not a lost one.
- Cadence via a vendor (a dunning provider, or Razorpay's account-level reminder schedule alone). `reminder_enable` is still set when notifications are on, but a vendor cadence is invisible to the audit trail and to the cap, cannot be voided when the order is paid, and would be the one part of the customer's experience the table does not govern.

Consequence: the simulation is unchanged (`scripts/simulate.py` never calls `schedule_job`), so `docs/simulation_report.md` says nothing about what the salary window, quiet hours, the cap or the reminders do to recovery; those effects are unmeasured until `insights` has real rows. The salary window is one number in `.env` because it is a guess about a population, not a fact about any customer. Reminders are hidden from plain job queries by an ORM loader criterion so that nothing that reads "the latest job" mistakes a voided reminder for the link's state.

### D9. Learning is a report a person reads

Decision: `app/insights.py` (`insights`, `make insights`, `GET /insights`) turns `outcomes` back into evidence: recovery rate among sent links per class and per scheduled-delay bucket with a 95% Wilson interval (written by hand; Wald says 3 of 3 is 100% +/- 0%, which is nonsense at this n), average time to recovery, LLM usage with every `fallback_taken` counted and latency percentiles, money at risk, recovered, open and parked per merchant, and one verdict per class. A change of delay is proposed only when the policy bucket and a competitor both have `n >= 30` and their intervals do not overlap; otherwise the line says `insufficient evidence (n=..)` with the number, which is what every class says on the demo database and what a test asserts. `app/rule_candidates.py` (`propose-rules`, `GET /rule-candidates`) does the same for the classifier: recurring model-classified descriptions with an agreed class at mean confidence >= 0.85 become a ready-to-paste `Rule(...)` stub, recurring fallbacks become "needs a human label", and a test checks `app/classify.py`'s hash before and after. `app/priority.py` uses the same `n >= 30` bar to decide when the expected-value estimate on the queue may replace the simulation prior with the measured rate, and names the source on every line. Nothing in any of this writes to `app/policy.py`, `app/classify.py` or a merchant file (`docs/insights.md`).

Alternatives rejected:
- A bandit over class x delay. An explore arm in a money path sends some customers a worse-timed link on purpose; the guardrail it would need is the table it would be replacing; and with n in the single digits per class it would be learning noise.
- Auto-tuning the table from the report. A proposal that applies itself is a policy change nobody reviewed; the reviewer weighs what the numbers cannot see (a merchant's salary cycle, an issuer incident that week, the double-charge risk of a shorter delay).
- Letting the model choose the delay per event, with the outcome as feedback. Non-deterministic, unauditable, and it would put a model answer on the money path, which D2 forbids.

Consequence: the agent has an audit table waiting for data, and it says so instead of guessing: every rate carries its n and interval, every expected-value figure names its source, and the first actionable proposal needs thirty sent links in a class. The human-queue share is the one signal read without a threshold on n: over 30% in a class that is not RISK_BLOCKED is flagged as a rules gap pointing at `propose-rules`.

### D10. Merchant configuration can only make the agent more conservative

Decision: `merchants/<merchant_id>.json` (plus an optional `_default.json`) is validated at startup by `app/merchants.py` against a closed schema (unknown keys and out-of-range values are rejected with the file and field named; the CLI and the operator view exit 1 rather than fall back to the defaults silently), and applied by `policy.effective_entry`, `pipeline.attach_nudge`, `scheduling.contact_cap` and `executor.build_payment_link_payload`. The invariants are enforced in `app/policy.py`, not only in the schema: `action` may only move to `human_queue`; RISK_BLOCKED and UNKNOWN stay parked whatever a file says; `max_attempts` is 0..5, `delay_seconds >= 0`, `max_contacts_per_week` 0..50; `notify_customer: false` always wins and `true` still needs the global flag; a disabled `token_retry` falls back to the class's link action and a disabled link action parks the class; `human_queue_all` is a kill switch; and the confidence, reachability and stop-rule gates run before any override. With no file the decision path is byte-identical to the table (`tests/test_merchants.py::test_no_overrides_is_byte_identical_to_the_table`). `python -m app.main policy --merchant ID` and `/policy?merchant=ID` show the effective table with the changed fields named (`docs/merchants.md`).

Alternatives rejected:
- A general rules engine or a policy DSL per merchant. Every new degree of freedom is a way to make the agent retry a risk flag or charge a token into an empty account; the review that keeps the table safe cannot be repeated per merchant file.
- Overrides in the database with an edit form in the operator view. The view is read-only and unauthenticated by design; a file is code-reviewed, diffed and deployed like the table it modifies.
- Symmetric overrides (a merchant may also shorten a delay or raise `max_attempts` past the table). The table's rows are the ceiling a payments reviewer signed off; a merchant can be more cautious than that ceiling without a review, and less cautious only by changing the table.

Consequence: a merchant file is loaded once per process and never mid-run, so the operator sees exactly what the next process start will do; the registry is in-process and directory-backed, which is fine for tens of merchants and is the second thing after the scheduler to become a table at thousands. The expected-value estimate for a parked attempt uses the class's own table action with the attempt's token state (at the decision's delay when a file changed it), because the queue orders what a person could recover by hand.

### D11. Trust before automation: shadow mode, offers as post-policy overrides, operator actions

Decision: three things a merchant needs before they let an agent touch their customers live in the same discipline as the rest. Shadow mode (`PRA_MODE=shadow`) runs every stage up to and including the atomic claim on the job row and then makes no outbound call: the job ends `shadow` with the exact payload in the audit trail, and `pra plan` totals what would have gone out with its expected recovery, so a merchant can import a month of failures and read the plan before switching on. Offers (partial payment on a large balance failure after the first link expired, a partial first link for a repeat balance failer, human queue for repeat hard declines or any risk history, UPI-first checkout on a dead card) are deterministic post-policy overrides in `app/offers.py`, applied after `policy.decide` like the subscription override, so the table stays reviewable and the offer is visible as its own audited step. Operator actions (`app/actions.py`) are the only writes the web view accepts, behind a token the operator types, through a confirmation page, each audited with the actor.

Alternatives rejected:
- A "dry run" that skips the database as well as the network. It would not exercise the idempotency insert, the claim or the timing rules, so the plan a merchant reads would not be the plan the agent executes. Shadow stops one step later, at the boundary, and nowhere else.
- Putting offers into the policy table as new actions. A partial link is still a `recovery_link`; making it a table action would double the rows and let a merchant file turn an offer on for a class where it is unsafe. As an override it can only run after the table's gates.
- A login for the operator view. A session system is a week of work and a new attack surface for a loopback tool; a typed token plus a confirmation page and an audit row per action gives attribution without either, and the doc says what a production deployment would need instead.

Consequence: shadow jobs are terminal and never reconciled or polled, so switching a merchant from shadow to live means their shadow rows stay as history and new events are processed fresh. The Payment Link fields the offers rely on (`accept_partial`, `first_min_partial_amount`, `options.checkout.method`) are assumptions; a 400 that names them falls back to a plain link under the same `reference_id`, audited. Pre-emptive recovery for expiring instruments was scoped and cut cleanly in the last round.

## Failure model

Faults are injected at the client boundary, never inside business logic, so `python scripts/chaos.py --all` exercises the exact path production would take; `docs/failure_report.md` is its output (9 of 9 pass) and `tests/test_chaos.py` runs every scenario under pytest.

LLM boundary (`app/llm.py`). Both call sites never raise. `classify_unmapped` makes one call, validates the JSON against a pydantic model whose `failure_class` is the closed `FailureClass` enum, makes exactly one repair call quoting the rejected output, then returns `Classification(UNKNOWN, source="fallback", confidence=0.0, fallback_taken=...)` with a stable name: `llm_unavailable`, `llm_timeout`, `llm_error`, `llm_bad_json`, `llm_hallucinated_class`, `llm_refusal`, `llm_truncated`, each `->human_queue`. `draft_nudge` applies the same one-repair rule plus content checks (channel matches, the exact amount string and the link appear verbatim and no other amount does, SMS body at most 300 characters when every character is Latin-1 and 200 otherwise, email subject at most 60, no fraud/risk/suspicious/suspect/blocked/blacklist words, no refund or guarantee promises in either language, no emoji, Devanagari present for `hi` and absent otherwise) and falls to `app/nudge_templates.py` in the same language (`...->template`); RISK_BLOCKED, UNKNOWN and any human-queued, no-action or token-retry job get the neutral template without a model call. The prompt for classification carries no name, contact or email; the nudge prompt carries the first name only; both system blocks are byte-stable and carry nothing about any customer. Invariant: a model failure is never a money action, and message generation never blocks the link it belongs to, because the nudge is drafted after that link exists; it is synchronous, so with a key each sent link adds up to the LLM bound to the pipeline's wall clock and delays the next job in a run-due sweep. Faults beat keys in `llm.get_client()`, so the chaos run never touches the network.

Razorpay boundary (`app/executor.py`, `app/razorpay_client.py`, `app/cadence.py`). `execute_job` commits `attempts_made` before every HTTP call (a crash mid-request leaves evidence), retries 429, 5xx and transport failures with `0.5s, 1.0s` between at most three attempts, treats any other 4xx as final, maps a duplicate `reference_id` to `sent` with the link recovered by lookup (or to `human_queue` when it cannot be), and otherwise marks the job `failed` with `last_error = <kind>: <detail>`. `finish_job` routes on the kind: `server_error` (Razorpay answered, so a recovery attempt) is reconciled by `reference_id` lookup first (found: `sent`; absent: next attempt on the class backoff; unknown: a person); `final_4xx` and `local_guard` go to a person. A delivery failure that never reached Razorpay's application layer — `rate_limited` (throttled) or a `transport` send a lookup shows created nothing — is separated from a recovery attempt: it re-sends after a short API backoff (`DELIVERY_RETRY_BACKOFF_SECONDS`) rather than the class delay, and if nothing ever reached Razorpay it is parked for a person instead of being closed `no_action: max attempts reached`, because "recovery exhausted" is the wrong close for a send that was never delivered (`tests/test_pipeline.py::test_rate_limited_on_a_max_one_class_is_parked_for_a_person_not_no_action`). The chain runs until the stop rule, at which point a `no_action` job and a not-recovered outcome are written so the payment is visibly closed. A reminder that fails at `notify_by` is marked `failed` with the kind and gets no chain of its own; a reminder is skipped `no_action` when the link is no longer open, when notifications are off, or when the order was paid, moved when its sweep falls inside quiet hours, and parked when the cap is hit. A cancel that fails parks the link job for a person. Invariant: at most one Payment Link per payment across the whole chain (reminders re-notify, they never create), a Razorpay outage produces zero half-created links, and a paid order produces zero new sends.

Database boundary (`app/pipeline.py:db_guarded`). Any `DBAPIError` becomes `PipelineDBError` after a best-effort rollback, `app/main.py` maps it to exit code 2 with "the event was NOT acknowledged ... redeliver it" on stderr, and because the job row is committed before the HTTP call, nothing has gone out. Invariant: nothing leaves the process before the job row with its key is durable; a redelivery after the outage meets the same key.

Duplicate delivery (`app/executor.py:schedule_job`). Webhooks are at-least-once. The second delivery is classified and decided again (deterministically, so the trail shows two identical decisions), then the INSERT under `sha256(payment_id:1)` fails, a `skipped_duplicate` audit row is written and the existing job is returned unchanged, whatever its status. Invariant: one job row, one link, one extra audit row.

The order-paid boundary (`app/ingest.py:record_order_paid`, `app/executor.py:paid_order`). A capture for an order voids what is pending, cancels what is live and refuses what arrives late (a stale `payment.failed` for the same order is `no_action` at schedule); the guard is read at schedule and again at execute, before any claim or call. Invariant: after the order is paid, zero create calls and zero token charges for it, one cancel call at most per live link, and a cancel that cannot be confirmed is a person's job, never a silent live link.

What the harness does not cover is stated at the end of `docs/failure_report.md`: the live HTTP paths (faults are injected one layer below them), two processes on one job at the same instant, and whether the real API rejects a duplicate `reference_id`, lists links by `reference_id`, cancels a link with `status: cancelled` and lists an order's payments as assumed (all stated in `FixtureRazorpayClient`; if the first does not hold, the worst case is a redundant link, never a second charge, because a Payment Link only collects money when a customer chooses to pay it; if the others do not, the reconcile step or the cancel parks the payment for a person). `PRA_FAULTS=<name>` injects any of the nine into a CLI run as well: `db_unavailable` binds the database to an unopenable path (exit 2), `duplicate_event` delivers every processed event twice, `unknown_error_code` forces the LLM off, `order_paid_elsewhere` pays every order that just got a link so every link is cancelled again.

## What I'd rebuild with another month

- The ingest path: `serve-webhook` exists (signature check, 500 and no acknowledgement on a refused write, so Razorpay redelivers) and now handles seven event types (`ingest.HANDLED_EVENTS`), but has only been driven by tests and local requests; next is running it behind a public URL against real test-mode deliveries and pinning every payload shape (failed, captured, paid, expired, order, subscription) and the reason vocabulary from what arrives, with `poll` becoming a fallback rather than the primary outcome source.
- The scheduler: Postgres and a `SKIP LOCKED` worker pool (or a queue fed by the same job rows), a lease on `execute_job`, partitioning by merchant, and Alembic now that the schema has a history.
- Token retries for real: Razorpay tokens and recurring payments behind the same `Action.TOKEN_RETRY`, with a mandate check before any debit and its own idempotency sequence; then `subscription.activated` in and pause/cancel out at the stop rule.
- Delivery of the drafted wording: an SMS/WhatsApp provider behind the stored `nudge_*` fields with consent and DND handling, joining the `deliver` rows Razorpay's own notification already writes; today the customer reads Razorpay's message, not the agent's.
- Rail-switch logic (UPI to card and back) as a new action in the table, which was the first thing cut.
- The rules: replay real `payment.failed` payloads against `app/classify.py`, and act on the first `propose-rules` candidates that recur in production rather than in fixtures.
- The Razorpay assumptions under the lost-state and paid-elsewhere paths: confirm against the real test API the duplicate-`reference_id` rejection and wording, the `reference_id` lookup, the cancel response, the order-payments list and `notify_by`; until then the code parks for a person whenever those answers do not come back as expected.
- LLM observability: persist the per-call `input_tokens`, `output_tokens` and `cache_read_input_tokens` that `usage_log` already holds in memory next to `llm_latency_ms`, and calibrate the 0.7 gate against human-queue verdicts rather than choosing it.
- The human queue as a queue: `resolve` records a person's decision and `queue` orders it by expected value, but there is no assignment, no notes history and no approval path that executes a held job.

## Panel prep

**Token cost and latency per event, and why no money moves while a model is thinking.** Not measured: no key was available on the build box, so what I can give is list price (`claude-opus-5`, the default `LLM_MODEL`, at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens on the June 2026 list; output is capped at 512 tokens, so at most about $0.013 of output per call) and the bound the configuration sets: 8-second timeout, one SDK retry, at most two calls per site, so on the order of 32 seconds worst case per site and about 64 seconds for an unmapped event that the model classifies into a link class and then nudges, before the SDK's own retry backoff. On the input side the classification system block is 3,635 bytes (roughly 900 tokens by a four-characters-per-token estimate) and is sent as one prompt-cached block that is byte-identical across calls, so after the first call its cost is a cache read rather than a full input charge; the nudge block is about half that and probably below the model's cacheable minimum. Per event that is under about 2,500 input tokens and 1,024 output tokens across both sites in the worst case, or under about $0.04 at list price before any cache discount: a bound derived from the code, not a measurement. The code records `input_tokens`, `output_tokens` and `cache_read_input_tokens` per call in memory and `llm_latency_ms` per decision in the database, so the real numbers, including whether the cache is actually hit, are one query and one log line away once a key is in `.env`. The rules run first at about 26 microseconds per event on this machine and the model is only asked for what fell through (3 of 22 seed events, 5% of the simulated mix) and for the nudge after the link already exists, so no money moves while a model is thinking.

**What breaks at 10,000 merchants.** The scheduler: `run-due` is one process scanning one SQLite table for due rows, links and reminders alike, executing them in order with real sleeps, no lease, no partitioning, and SQLite's single writer serialises every merchant. I would move to Postgres with a `SKIP LOCKED` worker pool or a queue fed by the same `schedule_job` rows, which is safe to make because the job row and its `reference_id` exist before any call: two workers on one job are refused by the status re-read or, if they race past it, by Razorpay's duplicate-`reference_id` rejection (assumed, not verified; a lease on `execute_job` is part of the migration). Two smaller things go with it: `/metrics` recomputes every series from the database on each scrape, which is a full scan per scrape and becomes a materialised counter set; and the merchant registry is a directory read once per process, which becomes a table with a cache once files are edited by more than one team.

**Why not retry everything at T+1h.** Blanket retries spend issuer attempts on cards that cannot authorise and on accounts that will not have money for two days, which raises the merchant's decline rate with the issuer, and a retry on a risk-flagged instrument is the behaviour that gets a merchant flagged. Without idempotency it also double-charges: in the simulation the baseline's 20 duplicate deliveries produced 17 duplicate links in 500 events under seed 42 (and double charges on token retries under other seeds), the agent produced zero of either in every seed, and that is the number a payments reviewer looks for first. And a T+1h cron has no idea the order was paid at T+30m; this agent cancels the link.

**What I'd rip out and rebuild given another month.** The scheduler and the ingest path, replaced by a webhook receiver feeding a real queue over Postgres, and the token-retry stub replaced by the recurring-payments API with a mandate check. I would keep the rules, the table, the layered idempotency and the paid-elsewhere stop, the timing rules, the chaos harness and the insights report, because a month of real traffic would refine those rather than replace them.

**Where the simulation is wrong.** The recovery probabilities in `scripts/simulate.py` are hand-picked, so the ordering (48 hours beats 1 hour for insufficient funds, minutes beat an hour for abandonment and outages) is the claim and the magnitudes are not; classification is assumed perfect, the human queue recovers nothing, duplicates arrive at the same instant as their original, wasted attempts are counted rather than priced, and none of the salary window, quiet hours, contact cap, reminders or the paid-elsewhere stop is modelled. The comparison holds across seeds 1 to 5 (recovered-count delta between +76 and +92, never negative), which is the only thing the simulation is entitled to say; the absolute 32.7% recovery rate is an artefact of the model and should not be repeated outside that caveat. The same hand-picked table is the prior behind every expected-value figure in `queue` until a class has thirty sent links of its own.
